Why Agile Fails in Traditional Manufacturing Environments

Introduce the topic here. Agile methodology, originally designed for software development, has become a buzzword across all industries. From marketing to human resources, everyone wants to "go agile." But what happens when you try to force two-week sprints into a physical supply chain?

The Problem with Physical Dependencies

In software, compiling code is nearly instantaneous. In manufacturing, curing concrete or waiting for specialized hardware from an overseas supplier is bound by the laws of physics. You cannot iterate a physical prototype on a two-week sprint schedule without incurring massive costs.

When applying Agile to hardware or heavy industry, Project Managers often face a disconnect between the sprint velocity expectations set by leadership and the reality of vendor lead times.

Enter the Hybrid Approach

Instead of strict Scrum, a Hybrid approach often works best. This involves maintaining a traditional Waterfall structure for procurement and physical construction phases, while utilizing Agile methodologies for the software integration and design phases.

Conclusion

Don't force a framework just because it's popular. Understand the constraints of your environment and adapt your PM tools to serve the project, not the other way around.
